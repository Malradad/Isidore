## iFVG — inversion fair value gap (his core setup)
- **Status:** ~ · videos studied: 8 · tested on price: ✓ v0.4 deep 1m 2025-01→2026-11, 1,264 trades, win 41.9%, PF 0.91, −$5,863 (no edge as coded; max 1 trade/day PF 1.05). `journal/backtests/2026-11-18-deep.md`
- **Market & timeframe:** NQ (also ES, gold).
  ✓ (maker, 2026-11-18) He finds gaps on the **30-second, 1-, 2-, 3- and 5-minute**
  charts. Seen on his screen: 2-minute. In videos: 3-minute examples.
  Bias from higher timeframes (1H–1M) and higher-timeframe PD arrays.
- **Background:** a fair value gap (FVG) is a three-candle imbalance. An inversion
  is a candle *closing* through it, so the gap flips from resistance to support (or the reverse).

### The A+ version (what he actually waits for)
1. **Start from a higher-timeframe level** (an HTF fair value gap / PD array). Setups
   that don't start from one are weaker even if they look clean.
   `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:03:17`
2. **One clean liquidity sweep** of an obvious level (e.g. old New York low), then a
   sharp **V-shape** reversal. One sweep, one move.
   `youtube/hd8OyKtQfx0 @ 00:00:34`
3. **The inversion right after the V.** A candle closes through the FVG. He enters
   on that close. `web/search-2026-11-18`, `youtube/DtX8ohvX4YQ @ 00:04:33`
4. **Strong candles, real volume** (Oct 2026 model): a big-volume candle against him
   gets absorbed by an even bigger candle that inverts the gap (a "delta flip").
   Pale/white low-volume candles are "worthless". `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:01:03, 00:04:36`
5. **Target engineered liquidity:** equal highs/lows, stacked highs, trend-line
   liquidity. Not a random single high. `youtube/hd8OyKtQfx0 @ 00:03:17–00:05:06`
6. **Direction matches his bias.** He ignores setups against it, even graded A.
   `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:02:27`

### His order of importance (Jul + Oct 2026)
**Target first, location second, inversion third, risk last.** `youtube/Rcf2t7ebjPg`, `youtube/KB4yG424nfk`
1. **Draw on liquidity — "fuel" (most important).** Engineered highs/lows that haven't
   been taken: lower highs that never took the previous high, equal highs/lows, trend lines.
   The target is the **top of the fuel** (the last high in the unbroken chain).
   A low that already took the low before it is *not* fuel.
2. **One-sided liquidity ("the holy grail").** Trade only after **one side's stops have been
   run and the other side's are still there.** Fuel on **both** sides → sit out, "trading is
   supposed to be boring."
3. **Location.** Enter in discount for longs (premium for shorts), near the sweep, not
   **overextended** after the move has already gone. Risk small relative to the target.
4. **The inversion + "what's to the left?"** It must come off a higher-timeframe gap / PD
   array (or demand zone / low-volume node). Use the timeframe where it's a single clean gap.
   Must **close** through; a wick doesn't count.
5. **Push-through ratio:** the number of candles it takes to invert the gap. 1 giant candle
   through 4 small ones is good; **more than ~7 candles → avoid** (mainly for beginners).
6. **Risk.** Same risk every time; no revenge trading; never move the stop away.
   "A professional takes an A+ setup, loses, and says I'd take that exact trade again."

**His four questions before entry:** Where is price trying to go? Is the iFVG located
somewhere that makes sense? Has price given enough confirmation? Is the risk worth it?
All four yes → enter. `youtube/Rcf2t7ebjPg`

**Timeframes (✓ matches maker):** iFVG on 30s–5m; 15m and above for bias only. Can drop to
30s for a tighter-stop confirmation entry. Don't use a higher-timeframe iFVG alone as bias.

### What makes it fail (his own "don't" list)
- **Repeated sweeps inside the same range:** the 2nd, 3rd, 5th sweep of a low
  makes each new iFVG worse. A sweep far *outside* the range is different.
  `youtube/hd8OyKtQfx0 @ 00:00:51, 00:02:42`
- **No engineered liquidity to target:** "looks good, V-shape, boom, completely failed."
  `youtube/hd8OyKtQfx0 @ 00:04:47`
- **No volume behind the inversion:** price dips back below the gap; close-based
  stops get hit. `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:02:43`
- **"YouTube price action" trap:** taking setups that don't look like the obvious
  textbook ones, at random times (e.g. 09:41 after the Judas swing already played),
  with no engineered liquidity. `youtube/zAZqCeckrwg`
- **Copying every signal:** "If you copy every signal, you will lose."
  `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:03:33`

### Entry, stop, target
- **Entry:** close of the inversion candle.
- **Stop — three versions, not one:**
  - ✓ (maker) a candle close back through the gap. He calls this "below the close,
    like I taught in previous videos." `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:02:59`
  - ✓ (maker) at the **swing low/high** of the sweep: survives a dip back through the gap
    when there's no volume behind the move.
  - ✓ (maker) **under the strong inversion candle** when volume is strong: tighter stop, better R.
    `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:05:24`
  - ✓ (maker, 2026-11-18) **Which stop when:** close-based by default; swing stop
    when volume behind the inversion is weak; stop under the inversion candle when
    volume is strong. The stop is chosen by the strength of the move.
- **Target:** the engineered liquidity. Example trade ~4R. `youtube/ETRextCcwCo @ 00:02:10`
- **Breakeven:** targeting engineered liquidity "gives good breakeven points."

## Mechanical model (his 2023 class) — testable as stated
Source: streams/2023-09-04 ErsWq6j87uY @ 00:18:15–00:55:53. ~
1. Mark the **previous day's high and low**. Never trade inside them.
2. Wait for one to be **taken** in London or New York (not Asia).
3. Entry: the **last single 5m (or 15m) FVG in the opposite direction** is closed through with displacement.
4. Stop: a candle close back through the inversion (or beyond the order block); hard stop if price wicks far.
5. Target: the next obvious internal high/low; scale there, runner to a larger pool.
6. If it already played out in London, no trade in New York. Size: 5 micros on 25K, 8 on 50K.
Unlike v0.4, this gives the **context** mechanically: a named higher-timeframe level (PDH/PDL) as the sweep,
a higher timeframe (5m/15m) for the gap, one setup per day.
