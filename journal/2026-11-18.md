## Test 2 — hotkey test (mechanics test, not a strategy trade)

Approved by the maker in chat. Paper account "Isidore", MNQZ2026 (MNQ1!), 1 contract. Times are New York (EST).

**Settings.** Trading settings (Paper Trading ▾ → Trading settings…) have **no default stop-loss or quantity option**. They only offer Buy/sell buttons, One-click trading (on), sounds and display options. Quantity came from the chart's quick-trade box, which was already 1. **No stop attaches automatically.**

| P&L | −2.00 pts = −$4.00 | +1.50 pts = +$3.00 |

Account after: $49,995.50 (realized −$4.50 across Tests 1–2). Positions are empty and Orders → Working is empty. Every bracket leg shows Cancelled in Order history.

**Quirks / lessons**
- Shift+B / Shift+S fire an **instant market order** with One-click trading on. There's no ticket and no confirmation, and the order goes out **naked, with no stop**. This is the biggest risk found: a stray keypress opens an unprotected position.
- The ~20–33 s edit times are mostly my own tool round-trips (screenshot → click → type → confirm), not TradingView lag. A human using the chart drag handles would be faster. Either way, the position sat **unprotected for 20–30 s** after each hotkey fill.
- To edit a stop or target: Positions tab → pencil → toggles + price fields → Confirm. The dialog shows the tick distance (160 ticks = 40 pts).
- Safer alternative to the hotkeys: use the order ticket with TP/SL toggles on (Test 1). It attaches the bracket in the same click, though offsets are measured from the quote, not the fill.
